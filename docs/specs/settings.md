# Settings spec · apps-engineer · M4

UI naming (renamed 2026-10-18, owner decision): the Desktop & Dock page section "Hot Corners" is "Screen Corners" (fr « Coins de l’écran »); the corner action "Notification Center" is "Notifications"; the app menu entry "System Settings…" is "Settings…" (fr « Réglages… »). Bindings descriptions "Next Space", "Previous Space", "Notification Center" of the bindings file are shown as "Next Desktop", "Previous Desktop", "Notifications" (`Bindings.uiName`).

## macOS reference behavior (described in words)
- Settings is one opaque window (740 x 690 in macOS 27): a sidebar on the left with a search field on top and
  one row per section (a 20-pt coloured icon tile with a white glyph, the name; the tiles are the vivid `color.tile` gradients, grey while the window is not key: ADR-0024 amendment 2026-11-03, `apps/settings/qml/TileGradient.qml`), a detail pane on the right with the
  title in the toolbar and grouped, rounded "inset" lists. Rows hold a label (optionally a grey caption) and a control on
  the right: switch, slider, pop-up menu, segmented choice. Changes apply immediately; there is no Apply button.
- macOS 27 layout (ADR-0024, measured on release captures, `docs/audits/ui-finesse-2026-10-25.md` section 7): the sidebar
  floats as an inset rounded panel (ADR-0024 amendment 2026-11-03: `window.sidebarPanelInset` 8, `window.sidebarPanelRadius` 8.7, concentric with the window's corner (ADR-0024 note 2026-11-04), a white rim), a 223-pt slot, 6 levels under the window (light #F7F6F3 on #FDFCF9; dark tokens
  `color.dark.sidebarFill` / `windowBackground`), with no dividing line; selection 195 x 32 (the 215-pt panel less 10 each side; measured), radius 8, 10 from both
  sidebar edges, in the selection colour with white text; a 28-pt capsule search field; groups #F7F7F7 without a border,
  radius 12, 468 wide with 20-pt margins, 10 apart, the first 12 below the toolbar; rows 38, labels and separators 10
  inside the group; section headers 13 bold in the text colour.
- Typing in the search field filters the sidebar. Up/Down in the sidebar switch the pane.
- Sidebar groups and hubs (PR #6, `docs/audits/settings-parity-2026-10-26.md`): the sections follow the macOS 27 order in
  groups with a 13-pt gap between them (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth | General, Accessibility, Appearance, Desktop & Dock, Displays, Menu
  Bar, Wallpaper | Notifications, Sound | Lock Screen | Keyboard, Mouse, Trackpad; Battery only on a laptop, Energy in its place on
  a computer without a battery or when UPower does not answer (macOS 27; `Power.paneFor`), Mouse only while a
  mouse is connected, Trackpad only where there is a touchpad). General is a hub: a header card (a
  48-pt tile, the name in bold, what it holds), then navigation rows (a 24-pt tile, the name, a chevron; 48 tall, 49
  apart) that open its sub-panes; the sidebar keeps General selected while one is open, the toolbar shows the sub-pane's
  title (none on the hub itself) and its back button returns to the hub. The search also finds the sub-panes. The search
  field and the toolbar's back / forward capsule have no outline, as in macOS 27 (ADR-0024 amendment 2026-11-03): the
  sidebar's search is a flat grey field, the back / forward capsule a white fill lifted by a soft shadow with a hairline
  between back and forward (`RimmedCapsule.qml`; Increase Contrast draws the control outline). A
  pop-up in a row is the macOS 27 one: the value as plain text, then a 20-pt disc with two chevrons (`ComboBox.inline`).

## macOS 27 parity inventory (owner brief 2026-10-26)
Target: every pane indistinguishable from macOS 27 Golden Gate System Settings on close inspection (F1 strict, ADR-0024),
except the accepted limits: Inter instead of SF Pro, Grassfield symbols, no lensing before phase 4, 1x screens, Grassfield
colours. Work lands in three pull requests, in this order: General, Hardware, Desktop.

**References.** The 512 Pixels Aqua Screenshot Library (Stephen Hackett) holds 31 macOS 27 System Settings captures at
2 px per pt (window 740 x 690 pt, sidebar 232). They are downloaded into `design/reference/macos27/` (git-ignored),
measured there, and never committed or redistributed; only numbers enter the repository. The macOS 26 Tahoe set lives in
`design/reference/macos26/`. Neither library has the General sub-panes (About, Software Update, Storage, Login Items &
Extensions, Language & Region, Date & Time, Sharing), Control Center or Screen Saver. For those panes the macOS 15 Sequoia
captures in `design/reference/older/` give the structure only: rows, labels and controls. The look comes from the macOS 27
panes, which share the group, row, header and control metrics. Every row below that uses a macOS 15 capture says so.
File names are given without the `27-Golden-Gate-Settings-` prefix (and `15-Sequoia-Settings-` for macOS 15).

**Sidebar (macOS 27, `General.png` and the scrolled captures `Keyboard.png`, `Printers-and-Scanners.png`).** The macOS
sidebar runs in groups separated by a gap:
1. Account
2. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Network, VPN, Battery
3. General, Accessibility, Appearance, Desktop & Dock, Displays, Menu Bar, Siri, Spotlight, Wallpaper
4. Notifications, Sound, Focus, Screen Time
5. Lock Screen, Privacy & Security, Touch ID & Password (Grassfield: Fingerprint & Key, ADR-0045), Users & Groups
6. Game Center, iCloud, Internet Accounts, Wallet & Apple Pay
7. Keyboard, Mouse (only while a mouse is connected), Trackpad (only where there is a touchpad), Printers (macOS: Printers & Scanners)

Grassfield keeps the same order and grouping, without the panes that have no counterpart (below). Today the sidebar is one
flat list of 14 sections, with About at the top level and no group gaps.

macOS 27 removed two panes and moved what they held:
- **Control Center:** its controls are now the "Menu Bar Controls" list of Menu Bar (`Menu-Bar.png`).
- **Screen Saver:** it is now a sheet opened from Wallpaper > Screen Saver… (`Wallpaper.png`).

Grassfield follows macOS 27 for both.

**Out of scope (no Linux counterpart, or Apple services):** Account, iCloud, Internet Accounts, Game Center, Wallet & Apple
Pay, Siri, Screen Time, AppleCare & Warranty, AirDrop & Continuity, AutoFill & Passwords, Startup Disk (the boot loader is
never touched), Time Machine (snapshots are never touched), Device Management, Transfer or Reset, and Users & Groups (no
account editing in 1.0). Touch ID & Password was out of scope as well (owner, 2026-10-26: "fingerprint enrolment belongs to
Omarchy's own setup, and changing a password is not a Settings action here"); ADR-0045 OC-0.8 (owner, 2026-11-04) supersedes
that for exactly what it adds: a Fingerprint & Key pane after Lock Screen (fingerprints enrolled and deleted through fprintd, the security
key through Omarchy's setup) and Disk Encryption's Change Password… (UDisks2). The account's own password is still not a
Settings action.

## Purpose and non-goals
Edit the user-facing keys of `grassfield.json` (contract version 1, master-prompt 5.3) and show read-only system
information. The feature is called "Settings" (code: `grassfield-settings`, app id `org.grassfield.Settings`).
Non-goals (v1): pairing a device that needs a PIN (Wi-Fi password entry and Just Works pairing: group 2, below), mirroring displays, Dock pinned apps (owned by the Dock),
user accounts, editing shortcuts. Wallpaper is a v1 section since ADR-0016 (local pictures, folders and colours, and the online Picture of the Day; video and per-display overrides are not in the pane yet).

   - Backends (GF-049; no new dependency: Qt D-Bus is part of Qt and already linked): Wi-Fi is read and driven over D-Bus through NetworkManager (`org.freedesktop.NetworkManager`, system bus), Bluetooth through BlueZ (`org.bluez`, system bus): `src/systembus.cpp` (one `BusCaller` door, so the logic runs against a canned bus in the unit tests and against fake services on a private bus in `tests/integration/settings_dbus.sh`), `Backend.busQuery` (one JSON state per page: `wifi.state`, `bt.state`) and `Backend.busAct` (an `{op: ...}` map), each job on a worker thread (`src/busjobs.h`) over the app's one system-bus connection, made on a worker and again when the bus went away, so a stalled service or a stalled connect never blocks the window; a job that does not finish in 10 s (60 s for an action: a few reads of 3 s, then the call itself, up to 30 s) answers "timed out", its late result is dropped, and at most 4 stuck jobs exist at a time. The jobs hold no pointer to the Backend: closing the window drops their results, waits at most 300 ms for a job in flight and abandons a stuck one (the process exits at once, after killing its child processes and with the application's own exit code, instead of staying alive until the call times out; `tests/unit/settings/tst_busjobs.cpp`, and `settings_dbus.sh` section 9 against a slow fake BlueZ); `apps/settings/qml/System.js` parses the JSON states and builds the action maps. A refresh only updates changed fields, inserts, removes or moves rows; a kept row is never rebuilt, so keyboard focus and the checked row survive.
   - Network names are untrusted bytes (newline, ':', backslash are legal): NetworkManager gives the SSID as bytes, which the Backend hands to the page as hex and `System.js` decodes; control characters show as U+FFFD, a name that is not clean UTF-8 or holds a control character is never passed on, and an open network is joined by its SSID bytes (at most 32). The security label is derived from the access point's flags (`System.wifiSecurity`: WPA3, WPA2, WPA1, 802.1X, OWE, WEP). A saved network is "known" when a Wi-Fi profile has that SSID (NetworkManager's connection settings, no secrets), whatever the profile is called; connecting uses the profile (`ActivateConnection`), joining an open one `AddAndActivateConnection` with an open Wi-Fi profile on the strongest open access point of that SSID (an open and a secured network of the same name are two rows: the choice is made on the access point's security); a secured or vanished network is refused with a reason. Enhanced Open (OWE: encrypted, no password) is treated as open, as macOS does (Apple's platform security guide, "Wi-Fi security with Apple devices", presents OWE as protection "on open networks" for "unauthenticated networks", supported from macOS 13; it documents no lock or password for it, so the row shows no lock and a click joins it): `System.wifiSecurity` still labels it "OWE" but `secured` is false, and the join adds `key-mgmt: owe` to the profile (NetworkManager needs it, there is no secret); an access point that also offers SAE, PSK or 802.1X needs a password. ### Pointer (ADR-0014, Settings > Accessibility > Pointer: size and colours)
- Contract: `pointer.size`, `pointer.outlineColor` (default `#ffffff`) and `pointer.fillColor` (default `#000000`; the packaged theme's own colours) (schema, defaults, `Store.js`, `SettingsStore`); the shared pure logic is `ui/Grassfield/Ui/PointerSize.js` (range, `normalize`, `snap`, `fromStored`, `setCursorRequest`, `launchEnvironment`, `retryDelay`) and `ui/Grassfield/Ui/PointerColors.js` (colour validation, keys, regeneration command, build scheduling, colour maths). A colour is `#rrggbb` (the Settings app writes it in lower case); a hand-edited value that is anything else (a name, `#rgb`, `#rrggbbaa`, a number, null) reads as the default in the shell and in the Settings app. A hand-edited value outside 24..96, a fraction (51.5), a string ("64") or a boolean reads as 24 in the shell (`SettingsStore` keeps the raw JSON value, `fromStored`) and in the Settings app; a valid integer off the slider step snaps to it (51 reads as 52).
- Live effect: `shell/platform/Pointer.qml` (the only place that talks to the compositor for this) sends Hyprland's own request `/setcursor <theme> <size>` on `Hyprland.requestSocketPath`, the request socket of the instance the shell belongs to (the nested one in a dev session; never another instance). The theme is derived from the stored colours only, never from the session environment (`PointerColors.themeFor`, `Pointer.baseTheme`): `grassfield-cursors` for the default colours, `grassfield-cursors-custom` for any other pair. The session environment (`HYPRCURSOR_THEME`, else `XCURSOR_THEME`; `config/hypr/grassfield/cursor.lua` and the `uwsm finalize` import, `devenv.sh` in dev) only says what the compositor has until the first answered request; a session that started with the custom theme in its environment and has default colours is sent back to `grassfield-cursors` at startup. `cursor.lua` always starts the real session with `grassfield-cursors` (it no longer picks the custom theme because it exists on disk: that made Reset Colour a no-op after a login, and showed stale colours after a hand edit); the shell switches to the custom theme as soon as it has read its settings and the stamp of the theme names the stored colours (cost: the packaged colours show for about a second at login). A theme that is not Grassfield's (a session that does not use these cursors) is kept as it is. Requests are throttled to one per `settings.pointerApplyMs` (leading edge, then the last value). Nothing is sent when the shell is not on Hyprland (no socket), when the session exports no cursor theme (one info line), or when the size is already the compositor's. No process, no polling.
- Delivery: one fresh socket per request (a Quickshell `Socket` whose connect failed never connects again, so it is replaced). A size counts as applied only when Hyprland answered `ok`; no connection, no answer within `settings.pointerAnswerMs`, or any other answer is a lost request, sent again after `settings.pointerRetryMs` doubling each time, at most `settings.pointerRetryMax` attempts; then the shell waits for the next size change or for Hyprland's `configreloaded` event (a reload resets the cursor, so the wanted size is sent again). `tests/shell/pointer_delivery` runs it against a fake request socket (late server, restart, no answer, other answer, retries running out, reload).
- Startup (real session login): `Pointer.size` is bound to the stored size; as soon as the settings file is read the shell sends it if it differs from the session's size (`HYPRCURSOR_SIZE`/`XCURSOR_SIZE` at the shell's start). Applications started outside Grassfield before that (autostart entries, the login manager) keep the session default until they are restarted.
- The hyprcursor theme is SVG, so the compositor takes any size of the range. The XCursor fallback (XWayland, GTK, Qt apps that read the theme themselves) is built at 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 by `branding/cursors/build.sh`; those clients use the nearest size.
- Compositor behaviour (measured nested, Hyprland 0.56): `setcursor` changes the size used for the next cursor image; the image already shown is not redrawn until the shape name changes (2026-10-19, `tests/compositor/cursor_refresh.sh baseline`: entering another surface that sets the same shape, e.g. desktop → Dock → desktop, all `default`, keeps the old size, because `IHyprRenderer::setCursorFromName` returns when the name is unchanged and not forced; the arrow → I-beam → arrow round trip draws the new size). Live redraw comes from the compositor capability `cursor-refresh` (gfc/1, plugin 0.7.0-cursor, `docs/specs/compositor.md` FR39-FR40): `hl.plugin.grassfield.cursor_refresh()` without argument (`Minimized.cursorRefreshCall`, the name the plugin registers) re-applies the shape Hyprland shows, `IHyprRenderer::setCursorFromName(m_lastCursorData.name, true)`, so the pointer on screen is drawn again at the new size with the same shape and at the same place; nothing while the pointer is hidden or when a client draws its own cursor surface; requests within 25 ms are folded into one trailing redraw. `Pointer.qml` sends it after each delivered size only when `Capabilities.has("cursor-refresh")`. Measured nested (`tests/compositor/cursor_refresh.sh`): a still arrow and a still I-beam go 24 → 48 → 96 → 24 px (heights ×1.95/×3.9, back to the identical first image), the pointer never moves. Without it (plugin absent, or a plugin that does not have it yet) nothing is sent and the new size shows at the next shape change (e.g. the pointer over a text field and back); the Settings pane does no shape trick (macOS keeps one pointer shape while the slider moves).
- Applications: running ones keep what they started with (XCURSOR_SIZE and XCURSOR_THEME are read once). Every application the shell starts through its own launch path gets `XCURSOR_SIZE`/`HYPRCURSOR_SIZE` set to the chosen size, and `XCURSOR_THEME`/`HYPRCURSOR_THEME` set to `grassfield-cursors-custom` while the custom colours are wanted or on screen, each only when it differs from the session's (`Pointer.launchEnvironment`, `PointerSize.launchEnvironment`): Dock tiles, Apps, Search, menus, files and desktop open (`Apps.launch`, `Apps.openWith`, `Apps.openDetached`, through `Apps.launchEnvironment`), Settings (`SettingsApp.qml`) and Glance (`GlanceApp.qml`); in a dev session this is the same path as the isolated homes. Not covered, by design: an application started by `gio`/the desktop environment over D-Bus (D-Bus-activated apps are started by the bus, with the bus's environment, not the shell's), applications started outside Grassfield (autostart entries, the login manager, a terminal) and toolkits that read the cursor theme from gsettings/xsettings instead of the environment. The shell never writes the systemd/uwsm environment, the owner's dotfiles or gsettings. Dev: `scripts/dev/devenv.sh` takes the session size from `.dev/config/grassfield.json` (`pointer.size`, else 24; `GRASSFIELD_CURSOR_SIZE` overrides), so a nested session starts at the stored size.
- What the pane footnote says, and what is true: the pointer changes while the slider is dragged when the compositor extension has `cursor-refresh` (otherwise the new size shows at the next shape change); apps whose pointer the compositor draws (for example foot) follow live; only apps that draw their own pointer keep the previous size and colours until they are quit and reopened.
- Colours: the Accessibility pane has "Pointer outline colour" and "Pointer fill colour" rows, each with a live preview of the arrow (`PointerPreview.qml`, the same path as `arrow.svg.in` on the 32 unit grid) and a colour well (`ColorWell.qml`: a round swatch that opens a popover with preset swatches, a saturation/brightness square, a hue slider and a typed `#rrggbb` field; free colours; keyboard: Space/Return opens, arrows on the square, Esc closes), and a "Reset Colour" button (disabled for the default pair) that writes both defaults. Writes while dragging are at most one per `settings.colorWriteMs`, the final colour always. Like macOS there is no contrast guard: any pair is allowed.
- Regeneration (`shell/platform/PointerTheme.qml`, driven by `Pointer.qml`): for the default pair nothing is built and the session theme is used. Any other pair: after `settings.pointerColorDebounceMs` of quiet the shell runs `bash <build.sh> --out <icons dir> --name grassfield-cursors-custom --outline #… --fill #…` (`PointerColors.buildCommand`: one argument vector, no shell, colours validated, the theme name fixed) in a `Process`: `/usr/share/grassfield/cursors/build.sh` installed, `GRASSFIELD_CURSORS_BUILD` in a dev session. build.sh recolours the SVG sources (the two placeholders), runs `hyprcursor-util --create`, packs XCursor at 24/32/48/64/96 px, and writes the stamp `colors` (one line, `#outline #fill`, lower case) into the theme and installs atomically (temporary directory `.NAME.build.PID.XXXXXX` in the icons directory, then rename). The theme on disk is identified by that stamp, never by a counter: the shell reads it at startup and after every build that did not finish cleanly (cancelled, failed), and the theme is `ready` only when the stamp names exactly the wanted colours (a theme left by an earlier session is used as it is when it matches, rebuilt when not; A, then B cancelled, then A again loads what the stamp says). The build runs under `setsid` (leader of its own process group) and is stopped as a group (`kill -s TERM|KILL -- -PID`): no renderer or packer outlives it; build.sh also stops its descendants and removes its work directory with retries on every way out. `build.sh --clean` (run once at startup, also done by every build and `--remove`) deletes at once the work directories whose PID is not running, so a killed build leaves nothing after the next start (a live build's directory is never touched). One build at a time: colours that change while it runs terminate it (SIGTERM to the group, SIGKILL after `settings.pointerBuildKillMs`) and the newest pair is built next; a build that runs longer than `settings.pointerBuildTimeoutMs` is killed. When the theme is on disk `Pointer.qml` sends `/setcursor grassfield-cursors-custom <size>` (a rebuilt theme keeps its name, so the request is sent again when the colours it was built for differ from what the compositor loaded) and the `cursor-refresh` redraw; until then, and after a failure, the compositor keeps what it has. "Reset Colour" (default pair), and the startup of a session whose environment names the custom theme while the colours are the defaults: `/setcursor grassfield-cursors <size>`, then, once the compositor has left the custom theme and the settings have been read (or 3 s passed without a file, `settings.pointerSettingsWaitMs`), `build.sh --remove` deletes the custom theme (only a directory that holds `manifest.hl`, only the fixed name). Icons directory: `GRASSFIELD_CURSORS_ICONS` (dev: `.dev/share/icons`, which `devenv.sh` already puts in `XDG_DATA_DIRS` and `XCURSOR_PATH`), else `$XDG_DATA_HOME/icons`, else `$HOME/.local/share/icons` (what hyprcursor and XCursor search); a dev session without `GRASSFIELD_CURSORS_ICONS` refuses to build (never the owner's `~/.local/share/icons`). A build takes about 1.8 s (measured: 8 threads, build.sh with 4 parallel jobs).
- Failure path: a missing tool (`hyprcursor-util`, `rsvg-convert`, `python3`), no build script (exit 127), no icons directory or a failed build gives a reason (the last line of build.sh's stderr, at most 160 characters, plain text), the pair counts as failed (not retried in a loop; any colour change tries again), the pointer is unchanged, and the state is written to `pointer-theme.json` next to `grassfield.json` (`{"state": "default|building|ready|failed", "outline", "fill", "reason", "code"}`, atomic, runtime state, safe to delete; `code` is `builder`, `tool:NAME`, `folder` or `failed`) which the Settings app watches (`PointerThemeStatus.qml`; it only counts for the colours it names). The pane footnote says what failed in the user's language from the code ("The pointer colours could not be applied: rsvg-convert is not installed. The pointer is unchanged."), and shows the tool's own English message on a separate line ("Details: …"), never inside the translated sentence; "Applying the new pointer colours…" while building. The pane has one preview of the pointer (next to Reset Colour), not one per colour row. The typed hex field of the colour well follows the colour however it changes (swatch, square, slider, the stored value); text the user is typing that already names the colour stays as typed.
- Missing keys: a `pointer.size`, `pointer.outlineColor` or `pointer.fillColor` removed from `grassfield.json` reads as its default in the shell after the next load (`SettingsStore.resetMissingPointerKeys`: the JsonAdapter would keep the old value), as in the Settings app, and the pointer follows.
- Compositor state is never taken from the environment: until the compositor has answered "ok" to a request of this shell (`Pointer.known`), its theme and size are unknown. Once the settings are read, the wanted theme and size are therefore always sent once at shell start, again when the request socket changes, and again after a `configreloaded`, even when the environment already names the wanted theme and size. So a Reset made while the shell was not running (the compositor still on the custom theme) is applied at the next shell start. The custom theme directory is removed only after that request was answered "ok" (a failed switch, a silent compositor or no socket leave it on disk; the retries and the next start try again). Measured with the fake request socket (`tests/shell/pointer_delivery`): fresh start with the default colours sends one `/setcursor grassfield-cursors <size>`, nothing is removed while it is unanswered or answered with an error, the directory goes after "ok".
- Limits: `pointer-theme.json` is not removed; a session without any cursor theme (no request is possible) keeps a custom theme on disk; XCursor-only clients (XWayland, GTK, Qt apps that read the theme from the environment) use the nearest of the built sizes. Applications started by Grassfield get the custom theme through the launch environment (`Pointer.launchEnvironment`); applications started any other way keep the packaged theme and never show custom colours (`cursor.lua` no longer selects the custom theme), while the compositor-drawn pointer (and clients that follow it) uses the colours at once.
- Typed colour: `#rgb`/`rgb` is accepted when the field is committed (Return or focus out) and expanded to `#rrggbb`, which is all that is stored; while typing only a complete six-digit colour is applied. The failure "Details" line is one line: the home folder written `~`, cut at 160 characters with an ellipsis.

## Known limitations
- Displays is read-only (no write path); Wi-Fi joins WPA/WPA3 personal networks but not 802.1X ones, and Bluetooth pairs only devices that accept Just Works (no PIN or confirmation code). The system panes were verified against recorded tool output and shims, not by acting on real hardware.
- Appearance offers Light and Dark only: `auto` is accepted by the schema but the shell treats it as dark.
- Terminal and Developer Apps (ADR-0045 OC-11): Omarchy's theme is one for both sessions, so the Omarchy session follows the
  Grassfield appearance too (its wallpaper included); the footnote says so. Omarchy's theme change is slow (seconds: it
  regenerates its theme files and thumbnails, and may install a VS Code extension from the network), so the terminals follow
  a moment after the appearance. Chromium's colour follows only with Omarchy's passwordless sudoers rule (`%wheel`, which
  Omarchy installs): it is a machine policy, and where that rule does not apply (a user outside `wheel`, the rule removed)
  the helper's own `pkexec` stand-in fails as if pkexec were absent, so no administrator password is ever asked for and
  Chromium keeps its colour. A user's own Omarchy `theme-set` hook that calls hyprctl gets the helper's failing `hyprctl`
  stand-in (Grassfield's Hyprland is never reloaded by a theme change).
  GTK apps of this session may show Omarchy's dconf colour scheme for an instant when it changes, before Grassfield's portal
  value (ADR-0035 decision 2b-13). The keys are `terminal.*`, not ADR-0045's `appearance.terminal*` (contract version 1). The
  pop-ups list every theme in both menus (no light/dark filtering). Nested run and screenshot: NOT VERIFIED (cloud session).

- The Keyboard list shows only literal bindings (no numbered Desktop switchers) and is not refreshed while the app is open.
- About runs `pacman -Q` and `lspci` once when the page opens (synchronously, 3 s timeout each); on systems without them the value reads "Unknown". The Grassfield version is the Settings app version (the shell's own version string is not exposed).
- Slider drags write the file on every step (atomic, small); the shell applies each step.
- The nested dev session has no titlebars (compositor plugin not loaded), so the window is borderless there.
- The desktop entry uses the stock `preferences-system` icon; swap to the Grassfield `gear` once the icon set is installed into an icon theme.
- Concurrent writers: the shell (`SettingsStore`) writes by merge. Setters record dirty keys, the file is re-read right before the write and only those keys are replaced, atomically; external edits made within the save delay survive, and keys outside the schema are preserved (the schema forbids them, but a writer never drops what it does not own). A fresh or empty file is created from the full defaults.
- The Settings app edits by parse and re-serialize (`Store.js`), which cannot keep integers beyond 2^53, duplicate keys or integer-like object keys. A file containing any of them is treated like a malformed file: read-only with a "cannot be edited safely" banner, file untouched. The schema has no such values, so only hand-made files are affected. The detection is shared (`ui/Grassfield/Ui/JsonSafety.js`, also catching duplicate `__proto__` keys and big integers written with a decimal point or exponent); the shell's `SettingsStore` applies it too: it never rewrites such a file (warns once, keeps the changes in memory, `writeBlocked`).
- Displays > Brightness of an external display (ADR-0045 OC-4) needs DDC/CI on in the display's own menu, the `i2c-dev` kernel module and read-write access to `/dev/i2c-*` (ddcutil's udev rule or the `i2c` group); some displays, TVs, docks and adapters have no DDC/CI, and then there is no row. The slider does not follow a change made elsewhere while the pane is open (the brightness keys, the display's buttons): the level is read again only when the display is selected or a drag ends. A slow display answers a write in up to about a second, so a drag lands in a few steps. Omarchy's command never sets less than 1 % (a drag to 0 reads back 1 %). Not verified on hardware: the cloud session that built it had no display and no i2c bus.
- Without the AT-SPI bus the Qt accessibility tree is not published (Accessible properties are set but only verified by code review, not by a screen reader).
- ADR-0045 (OC-0.7, honesty rule): what no system service exposes still opens Omarchy's own script in Omarchy's terminal, behind a macOS 27 sheet that says why: the first fingerprint setup and its removal (fprintd's install and the PAM edits), the security key's setup and removal (pam-u2f, the key's registration), Remote Login's first setup (OpenSSH, the firewall rule, the authorized key), hibernation's setup and removal (the swap file, initramfs and boot entry), Dictation's install, model and removal (voxtype's model picker is interactive; Settings does not edit voxtype's own configuration), and the disk password when UDisks2 does not answer. Those are terminal windows, not Grassfield surfaces.
- Remote Login's switch makes two polkit requests the first time (systemd's manage-unit-files, then manage-units) unless polkit keeps the first answer; no daemon reload follows (a third request): the unit file state is read from the disk. Turning it off stops the server but leaves Omarchy's firewall rule and authorized keys (Omarchy's removal script is not used).
- Disk Encryption follows the root file system to its LUKS device only when one lies directly under the other (no LVM or other layer between them is followed); otherwise Change Password… opens Omarchy's terminal. UDisks2 keeps libblockdev's key derivation defaults, not Omarchy's `--pbkdf argon2id --iter-time 2000`.
- Fingerprint: the lock screen shows nothing of fprintd's conversation (it runs beside the password silently and unlocks on a match); the polkit sheet (shell/security/polkit, unchanged by ADR-0045) shows fprintd's lines as its grey note (a failed match in red) while its Allow button waits for the password prompt that follows the reader; a password typed and sent before that prompt is emptied, not sent. After three failed matches the lock screen stops using the reader until it locks again.
- Hibernation is in Battery and in Energy (a desktop without a battery), the same group (`HibernationGroup.qml`); Energy has no Options… button, so the group is its last.
- Dictation: while voxtype is installed, Ctrl-Super-X is Grassfield's even with Dictation off (it then does nothing); Omarchy's install notice still names F9, which the Grassfield session does not bind.

## ADR-0045 design review (nested, 2026-11-05; rule: closest to macOS 27, no technical step for the end user)

- Energy has Hibernation (a desktop hibernates too): `HibernationGroup.qml`, one component for Battery and Energy, after the other groups. Its state is read when the pane starts and when Settings is active again.
- Sidebar name: "Fingerprint & Security Key" was cut ("Fingerprint & Security …", fr "Empreinte et clé de sécu…") at 1x and 1.25 in a sidebar that fits about 157 px of text (measured with Inter 13, weight 400: "Confidentialité et sécurité" 157 px fits, "Fingerprint & Security Key" 159 px, fr "Empreinte et clé de sécurité" 170 px do not). Decision: **"Fingerprint & Key"** (105 px) and fr **"Empreinte et clé"** (98 px), for the sidebar row, the toolbar title and the card; no wider sidebar, no elision. macOS keeps its names short too.

- Keyboard > Dictation: the model is Voxtype's speech model, so the row "Speech model" has the button "Speech Model…" (fr "Modèle vocal…"), as the sheet "Choose a Speech Model".
- Sharing: the remote login command goes on a line of its own after "To log in to this computer remotely, type:", unquoted, so the wrap never breaks inside it and it stays one copyable string.
