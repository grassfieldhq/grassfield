# dock spec · shell-engineer · M1

## macOS reference behavior (from design/reference/, described in words — no Apple images committed)
Measured in F1 (docs/testing/f1-reference-gaps.md, Dock): a floating rounded-rectangle glass shelf (radius about 24, not a capsule) about 6 pt above the bottom edge, a little lighter than its backdrop with a visible light rim along the top edge, strongly translucent over the wallpaper; icon body about 0.735 of the pitch; a small dark dot under running apps; a hairline separator before the folders and the Trash; a label capsule with a downward tail above the hovered icon; an icon menu (rounded panel with a tail) above the icon. Icons magnify only when the option is on (off by default).

## Look (fidelity pass, 2026-10-19)
The shelf is `Material` with the Dock overrides: tint alpha `dock.tintAlphaDark` / `dock.tintAlphaLight` 0.05, clear glass (ADR-0024 section 2 point 1, G-7; it was 0.52 / 0.42: not the A2 text clamp, the shelf holds icons only, the labels are separate capsules on the global material), highlight `dock.highlightOpacity` (0.34) and Material's darker outer edge (ADR-0024 section 2.5, ADR-0039 step 25; the white outer rim `dock.rimOpacity` 0.34 is gone), always over a real compositor blur region (`BackgroundEffect.blurRegion`, nothing faked). Reduce Transparency still makes the shelf opaque (the override applies only while the material is not opaque). Radius `radius.dock` 24, padding `dock.padding` 10, separator `dock.separatorHeight` 49, spacing 7. Geometry per docs/audits/visual-tahoe-2026-10-25.md § Dock (ADR-0024 lot 5, C-15): the shelf is the icon plus its padding (68 for a 48-pt tile; `DockModel.shelfHeight`), with no band under the icons, so the body has the same room above and below; the running dot sits in the bottom padding, centred `dock.dotCentre` (6, macOS 27) from the shelf's edge; the separator slot is the hairline plus `dock.separatorGap` (8) on each side (an app body 18.5 from the line, macOS 27; docs/audits/ui-finesse-2026-10-25/f1-macos27.md), its hairline `colors.separator` at `dock.separatorOpacity` 0.3 (about a third darker than the shelf) and centred on the shelf; a label's tail tip sits `dock.tooltipGap` (8) above the shelf, or above a magnified icon that rises past it. The Trash uses the icon theme (`user-trash` empty, `user-trash-full`) in the user's icon mode (`TintedIcon`, ADR-0014); without those theme icons it falls back to the symbolic glyph on a rounded tile. The menu tail is `dock.menuTailWidth/Height/Gap` (19 / 13 / 16).

## Purpose and non-goals
Pinned, running and recent apps, Trash, launch/focus/cycle, launch bounce, badges, drag to reorder/remove, file drops, magnification, autohide, item context menu. Folder stacks (requirement 27), recent documents (28) and shrink to fit (26) are in; folders in the Dock are not a file manager (no drop-to-copy into a stack, no per-item menus in a stack).

## Functional requirements
1. One layer-shell window per screen, namespace `grassfield-dock`, anchored to the Dock edge (requirement 23: bottom+left+right, or left/right+top+bottom for a side Dock; transparent), exclusive zone = shelf thickness + margin on that edge (0 with autohide). Input is restricted with a mask to the shelf (plus the magnified area while hovered), or to a 2 px bottom strip while hidden.
2. Items (`DockModel.js`, pure): pinned desktop entry ids in the user's order (unresolvable ids skipped, duplicates merged), then running apps that are not pinned (foreign-toplevel app id mapped to an entry; case-insensitive), a separator, the Trash. Windows are never matched by title.
3. Running indicator dot under apps with at least one window (in the shelf's padding, see above): it follows the appearance (macOS: pure white in dark mode, pure black in light mode, `colors.indicator`), and it reads by its own halo (ADR-0024 G-7): a ring `dock.dotHalo` (1.5) wide of the opposite colour (black under the white dot in dark, white under the black dot in light) at `dock.dotHaloOpacity` 0.5, so the dot keeps 3:1 or more over ANY backdrop on the clear shelf, wallpaper or window (worst case 3.98:1 in dark over white, 5.3:1 in light over black; tests/unit/tst_bartone.qml). tests/visual/contrast.sh `dock` (nested) measured the dot against the shelf; it has not been re-run with the halo. No wallpaper sampling is needed.
4. Click, Return, Space: not running → `DesktopEntry.execute()`; running and not focused → activate the most recently focused window of the app (and move it back from `special:hidden`); running and already focused → cycle to the app's next window. Trash → `gio open trash:///`.
5. Tooltip with the app name above the icon (hover or keyboard focus), via the `Tooltip` component. The label appears after `dock.tooltipDelay` (250 ms) on an icon (at once for keyboard focus and the drag Remove capsule, and for the next icon within `dock.tooltipWarm` (400 ms) of a label), and an icon keeps its hovered state `dock.hoverHold` (80 ms) after the pointer leaves it, so crossing the gap between two icons does not flicker.
6. Magnification (`Magnify.js`, pure): falloff = quintic smootherstep, scale(d) = 1 + (max-1)·(1 - S(d/R)), S(t) = 10t³ - 15t⁴ + 6t⁵, for pointer distance d < R, R = `dock.magnificationRadius` (3) icon pitches; max = `dock.magnification` (default 1.6, clamp 1-2.5); disabled when `dock.magnificationEnabled` is false (default). The target scale of a tile is the mean of the curve over its own slot (tile plus half the spacing each side; closed-form integral, `Magnify.slotScales`): the slots tile the line, so the row is displaced by the integral of the curve, tile boxes never overlap along the Dock (the drawn icons grow across it, away from the screen edge, beyond their tile box: see "What takes the pointer") and nothing jumps at a slot boundary; separators stay at 1. The displayed scales follow the targets with an exponential smoothing in real time (`Magnify.step`, time constant `dock.magnificationTau` 26 ms, one frame step capped at `dock.magnificationMaxDt` 33 ms, driven by a `FrameAnimation` that runs only until settled; Reduce Motion snaps). Without the pointer the targets are 1, so the row settles back fully. A magnified icon also rises by `dock.magnificationLift` (0.1) of its growth, iconSize·(scale-1) (estimate: no macOS capture under design/reference to measure it; the running dot stays on the shelf). The layout is the unmagnified one, scaled; the shelf stays centred.
7. Autohide (`dock.autohide`, default false) is a state machine (`AutoHide.js`, pure; the Dock steps it from a `FrameAnimation` that stops while nothing can change). `visible` runs 1 to 0 and positions the shelf (eased with a smoothstep); Reduce Motion caps the two slide durations at `motion.reducedMax`.
   | State | Event | Next |
   |---|---|---|
   | shown | no inhibitor for `dock.hideGrace` (250 ms; the timer restarts whenever an inhibitor is set) | hiding |
   | hiding | any inhibitor, the pointer included (the slide reverses from its current position, no jump) | revealing |
   | hiding | `visible` reaches 0 after `dock.hideDuration` (300 ms) | hidden |
   | hidden | drag, popup, hold or keyboard inhibitor (never waits) | revealing |
   | hidden | the pointer on the edge strip (`dock.revealZone`, 2 px) for `dock.revealDwell` (120 ms) | revealing |
   | revealing | `visible` reaches 1 after `dock.revealDuration` (240 ms) | shown |
   Inhibitors that keep the Dock shown: an attention request (`attention`, `dock.attentionReveal` after the request), the pointer over it, a drag in progress (`dragFrom >= 0`), a Dock popup or menu (`Popups.kind == "dock"`), a press and hold on a tile, keyboard mode, and the test reveal hook. While hidden only the edge strip takes input (the input mask), so the pointer there means "at the edge". With autohide off the machine is shown at once. The hiding delay replaces the single 450 ms `dock.hideDelay` (removed). Fixture hook: `qs ipc call dock hidestate <screen>` prints "state visible".
8. Context menu (right click, or the Menu key on a focused item; `shell/overlay/DockMenu.qml`, a panel with a tail pointing at the icon): a running app's menu starts with its windows (macOS: only the windows of that tile, the same identity as the running dot; most recent first; titles as plain text collapsed to one line and elided at `menu.windowTitleMax` 40 like the menu bar's Window menu, the app name for an empty title; windows whose visible (already elided) titles are equal (two terminals) are told apart by " (2)", " (3)": a window keeps its number while it lives and a lone window shows none (`Launch.menuWindows`), owner report 2026-10-25; a check on the most recently focused window that is not minimized; "(minimized)" after the title of a minimized one) and a separator (`Launch.menuWindows`, pure, `tests/unit/tst_docklaunch.qml`); choosing one activates that window alone (`Compositor.activateAfterLayer`: Hyprland refuses to focus a window while an Exclusive layer surface exists, so it waits for `closelayer` of `grassfield-overlay`, at most `menu.layerWait` ms; `Compositor.activate` then restores it when minimized and switches to its Desktop, as a Dock click does) and nothing hands the focus back to the previous window (`FocusReturn.skip`). Then Open / New Window, the desktop entry's actions (`DesktopEntry.actions`), Options (macOS model: a running app has Keep in Dock, checked while kept, unchecking removes it once the app quits; a kept app that is not running has Remove from Dock and no Keep in Dock; a recent app (not running, not kept) has Keep in Dock and Remove from Dock, the latter forgetting it from `dock.recent` so it only returns when it is launched and quits again (owner report 2026-10-23; `Launch.optionRows`, pure, `tests/unit/tst_docklaunch.qml`); Open at Login, checked while its autostart file exists; Show in Files), Show All Windows (`Compositor.bringAllToFront`), Hide (`Compositor.hideApp`), Quit (close all windows of the app); an app that is not running has none of these three rows (macOS omits them, it never greys them out; lot 5). Option (Alt) variants follow the Alt key live while the menu is open (macOS; the menu holds the keyboard; `PopupMenu.altHeld`, set from the Alt key's own press and release so a submenu with the focus still reports it): Hide becomes Hide Others (`Compositor.hideOthers`, disabled when no other app has a visible window) and Quit becomes Force Quit, which asks "Force quit “App”?" / "Unsaved changes in this application will be lost." with the same destructive confirmation and kill path as the Grassfield menu's Force Quit… (`Popups.confirm` action `forcequit`, `Power.run`, `kill -9`) on the distinct client PIDs of the app's windows (`Launch.forceQuitPids`: unknown, init, negative PIDs are dropped; nothing is killed without PIDs). Open at Login writes or removes `<autostart dir>/<entry id>.desktop` (`AppLaunch.autostartFile`/`autostartText`: values escaped per the Desktop Entry spec, control characters in Exec refused, plain file names only; written by `overlay/write-autostart.sh`, temp file + rename; in a dev session `<dev>/apps-home/config/autostart`, never the owner's). Show in Files opens the folder of the entry's executable (`command -v`, `readlink -f`) through `Apps.openDetached`.
9. Trash icon: the theme's `user-trash` / `user-trash-full` (fallback `trash`/`trash-full` from branding), by `~/.local/share/Trash/files` emptiness (`find` at start and on hover, then on every `gio monitor` event). Its menu (right click or the Menu key; a press-and-hold still clicks) is the macOS one: Open (`Trash.open`) and Empty Trash… (disabled while the Trash is empty), which asks "Are you sure you want to permanently erase the items in the Trash?" / "You can’t undo this action." (destructive confirmation, Cancel focused, action `emptytrash` → `Power.run` → `Trash.emptyNow`). Emptying is the only deletion in `Trash`: a real session runs `gio trash --empty`; a dev session (`GRASSFIELD_DEV=1`) NEVER does (gio's trash:/// is served by the owner's session) and removes only the contents of the isolated trash `<dev>/apps-home/data/Trash/{files,info}` with `find -mindepth 1 -delete`, and nothing runs when the apps-home path is not a plain absolute `.../apps-home` (`AppLaunch.emptyTrashArgv`, `tests/unit/tst_applaunch.qml`; ADR-0011).
10. Keyboard: the dock takes keyboard focus only in keyboard mode (IPC `dock keyboard`, to be bound to a shortcut by the integrator): Tab/Shift+Tab move, Return opens, Menu key opens the item menu, Esc leaves the mode. Focus ring is 2 px accent and is drawn only in keyboard mode (a mouse click on a tile never shows it).

11. Minimized section (ADR-0002, master-prompt 5.6): right of the separator and before the Trash, one tile per window minimized by the shell (`Compositor.minimized`, newest last). Tile = app icon; in the `full` material profile a live `ScreencopyView` thumbnail with a small app-icon badge. Tooltip and accessible name: window title, ", minimized". Click, Return or Space restores the window to the current workspace and focuses it. Clicking an app icon whose windows are all minimized restores the most recent one; cycling skips minimized windows while the app has others. Closed windows leave the section.
12. Minimize paths: titlebar button (the plugin posts `grassfield>>{"type":"minimize-request","address":"…"}`; the address is validated as hex and mapped to a toplevel), Window menu, IPC `windows minimize` (bound to SUPER+M). `Compositor.minimize()` uses `hl.plugin.grassfield.minimize(address, x, y, w, h)` with the rectangle of the window's own tile (`Compositor.dockRect`: the Dock of the window's monitor, slot = separator + 1 + the window's index in the minimized list, global layout coordinates; each per-screen Dock registers with `Compositor.registerDock` and unregisters on destruction) when `Capabilities.has("minimize")`; otherwise it moves the window to `special:minimized`. Both branches record the window in the shell. `unminimize()` calls the plugin `restore(address, x, y, w, h)` toward the window's current tile on the focused monitor's Dock, or moves the window to the focused workspace, then activates it.

13. Launch and activation (owner report 2026-10-20, macOS 26 model; pure rules in `shell/dock/Launch.js`, tests `tests/unit/tst_docklaunch.qml`, nested `tests/visual/dock-launch.sh`):
    - Click on an app with no window: the icon hops until the app has a window or `dock.launchTimeout` (12 s) passes (`DockModel.bounceKeys`). One hop rises `dock.bounceHeightScale` (0.34) of the icon height and takes `dock.bounceDuration` (500 ms): ease out going up, ease in coming down. When the window appears the icon finishes its hop by landing (an OutBounce of `motion.normal`), the tile survives the rebuild because the tiles are a keyed list model (17). The first lifted frame is 20 ms after the click (measured in the shell). No bounce with Reduce Motion (the running dot still appears).
    - Press states, as implemented (`Dock.qml`, the left `TapHandler` of a tile):
      - While the button is held the icon is darker by `dock.pressDim` (0.25, brightness, offscreen layer only while pressed); the label goes away on the press and stays away until the pointer leaves the tile. The notification badge is a sibling of the icon, so the press-dim layer cannot clip it, and it hops with the icon.
      - Click: a left press released before `dock.menuHold` (550 ms) is a click. App and Files tiles: a press held past `dock.menuHold` opens the tile's Dock menu (macOS) and its release launches nothing (`menuByHold`). A press that turns into a drag is a drag (the drag cancels the tap handler, so no menu and no click).
      - Tiles without a Dock menu (Apps, Trash, minimized windows; the Trash has a menu on right click and the Menu key only, see 9) keep the click on release however long the button is held (`dock.clickHoldLimit`, 3600 s: Qt drops `tapped` after its long press, so these tiles get a limit they never reach).
      - Hold menu and drag (macOS: after a press-and-hold menu the rest of that press belongs to the menu): a press that opened the hold menu never moves a tile. The decision is taken when the drag handler would activate, from per-press state: `tile.menuByHold`, and `root.dragInterruptions` against the mark taken when the button went down (every interruption event of the drag-out list, see below, increments it). If either says so, the drag does not start: `win.dragFrom` stays -1, so nothing moves, no gap opens, the tooltip is unchanged, and the release changes no pin (the shell logs `drag-refused`). It is not an `enabled` guard on the drag handler: the tap handler cancels at the drag threshold (`pressed` becomes false), which would have re-enabled such a guard in the same event. The state is reset by the next press (`menuByHold` false, a new mark), so a deliberate drag on a later press, including one right after a hold menu was dismissed, reorders and unpins as usual. Nested trials (`tests/visual/dock-drag-interrupt.sh`): `holdmenu_then_drag_out` (release above the Dock) and `holdmenu_then_drag_sideways_reorder` (a running tile dragged along the shelf to before the pinned tile): pins unchanged, no `drag-start`, a logged `drag-refused`; `holdmenu_then_next_press_removes`; `interrupted_before_drag_sideways_refused` (the press is interrupted before the drag moves: refused). Fixtures IPC: `dock forcehold true` marks every press on an app tile as having opened the hold menu when it goes down, `dock forceinterrupt true` as interrupted, so the order (menu or interruption first, drag second) is deterministic.
      - An interrupted drag changes no pins: if an interruption arrives while a drag is active (a surface, popup, window, focus or workspace event, the lock, a cancelled grab), the drag is marked interrupted and its release, real or synthesized by Qt (a surface that takes the pointer makes Qt end the drag with a release that looks real; other interruptions leave the drag running until the user releases), never removes or reorders: `finishDrag` glides the tile back (`win.dragInterrupted`; it used to reorder). Nested trial `interrupted_during_drag_sideways_no_reorder` (Search opened 1.5 s into a sideways drag of a running tile: no reorder).
      - Right press (app and Files tiles): opens the Dock menu on the press, whatever the duration.
      - Cancelled click: a press that is taken away from the Dock never clicks. Qt then fires a tap with the button still down, so the Dock refuses it (`tap-refused-interrupted`). The cancelling events are a short explicit list, `Dock.qml` `root.cancelClicks()`, separate from the drag-out list (which over-cancels on purpose, owner batch 7): a shell surface opening over the Dock (Search, Overview, Apps, the switcher, a Dock or menu-bar menu, Notification Center, Control Center: `FocusReturn.surfaceOpened`, `Popups.kind` switching to a popup), the session lock, and Qt cancelling the grab (`TapHandler.canceled`, a cancelled grab on the handler). Window title changes and focus changes (`activewindowv2`) do NOT cancel a click (nested trial: eight clicks while the focused window retitled every 20 ms all tapped), and neither does a window opening or closing while the Dock still holds the press. But a window OPENING makes Qt end a held press by itself (a window closing never did: a 250 ms click while a window closed tapped, 3 of 3): the handler sees `pressed`, the window insertion (`objectInsertedPost` of the toplevel list), 35 ms later a tap, `pressed false` and a grab change with point state 8 (Released), no hover or cancel event, nothing that tells it from a real release while the button is still down. That release is not a click (macOS: a press the Dock did not finish does nothing): a press that began before a window insertion and whose tap comes less than `dock.clickWindowGuard` (150 ms) after it is refused (`tap-refused-window-event`), so neither a tap, an activation nor a menu happens and the user presses again. A real click that coincides with a window opening within 150 ms is lost the same way and costs one repeated click (for instance the second click of a fast double launch is lost when the first app's window maps while that click's button is down: 2 of 19 in the nested trial) (nested trial `mapdrop`: Trash and an app tile held 1 s with a window opened 0.1 s in).
      - Known limit: the hold menu is decided by a GUI-thread timer (`longPressThreshold`). When the Dock's thread stalls for 550 ms or more during a press, the timer can fire before the release is processed and a short click becomes the menu; Qt gives the handler no event timestamp to compare, so this cannot be told apart. The nested trials treat a press that the shell itself measured at 500 ms or more as a host stall and repeat it.
      - Known difference: on macOS you can press, hold, drag onto a menu item and release to pick it; here the menu is a separate popup that takes the pointer after the hold and the press is not handed over, so the menu items are picked with a second click. Ctrl+click is not handled (no Dock menu on it).
    - A second click on a tile that is launching does nothing (`Launch.click`: no second process), until a window appears or the timeout passes.
    - Click on a running app, `dock.clickMinimizes` on (default): the frontmost app (it owns the focused window, on this Desktop) has every visible window of this Desktop minimized, full-screen windows and Split View members excepted (`Launch.clickPlan`; if none can be minimized the click brings the app forward, never nothing). A click-minimize always goes INTO the app icon (`Compositor.minimizeIntoAppIcon`, whatever `dock.minimizeIntoApp` says): no tile is added, so the Dock does not move under the pointer and the next click on the same spot brings the windows back. When nothing of the app is shown on this Desktop and some windows are minimized, that click restores all of them (the most recent focused, tile group forward, TL29) and stays on this Desktop, even when the app has a window on another one. Only the pointer minimizes: keyboard activation, Accessibility press and the Dock menu bring forward. Minimizes from elsewhere (yellow light, Super+M) follow `dock.minimizeIntoApp`. With `dock.clickMinimizes` off (macOS):
- Click on a running app: every visible window of the app on the focused Desktop comes forward, the most recent last so that it is focused and in front (`Launch.activationPlan`); a click on the focused app does not cycle its windows. Windows of other Desktops stay where they are; when all its windows are on other Desktops, the most recent one is activated and the compositor switches to its Desktop (`Compositor.activate`; nested trial: Desktop 3 from Desktop 2). When all its windows are minimized, the most recent one is restored (the others stay minimized). An app tile with no window (a pinned or recent app that quit) is launched again through its desktop entry (single-instance apps open a new window). The same `bringForward` serves `qs ipc call dock reopen <desktop id>`, which a running single-instance app launched again calls when its window is not on screen (macOS: launching a running app brings it forward, a minimized window back; xdg-activation alone leaves a window on `special:minimized`); an app the Dock has no window for is left alone, and nothing happens while the session is locked (`DockModel.windowsOf`, `tst_dockmodel.qml`; Activity Monitor, ADR-0038 AD34).
    - The new window has the keyboard focus because Hyprland focuses a window when it maps (nested trial: the window of a Dock launch is the active window). The shell passes no `XDG_ACTIVATION_TOKEN`: Quickshell 0.3 has no API to create one (no activation type in the qmltypes); a launch in a real session through `uwsm-app` gets no token either. Limitation: an app that opens its window more than a few seconds after a click while the user has moved on to another window is not forced to the front.
    - Window animation (`config/hypr/grassfield/looknfeel.lua`): a new window scales from 94 % and fades in over 0.2 s (`windowsIn`, speed 2, `popin 94%`), closing is shorter (0.12 s, `popin 96%`).
    - Dev hooks (fixtures only): IPC `dock tiles` (keys in order); the shell logs `dock-launch: lift <ms since click> <px>` (bounce curve), `dock-launch: pressed true|false <epoch ms>`, `tap` and `menu-by-hold`, which `tests/visual/dock-launch.sh` reads (press and release times are the shell's, not guessed).
14. Badges and progress: a red count bubble at the top right of an app icon. By default the count comes from the notification history (`DockModel.badgeCount`: entries whose app name is the tile's name or entry id, case-insensitive; "99+" above 99). An application that sets its own count through `com.canonical.Unity.LauncherEntry` (ADR-0018, menu-bridge MB15; Chromium and Electron apps, Telegram, Thunderbird) owns its badge (macOS: the app is authoritative): `DockModel.tileBadge` uses that count and ignores the notification-derived one for that app while its own is known; count 0 (or `count-visible:false`) hides the badge; an app that never sends a count keeps the derived one. The app is matched on the tile's key or desktop entry id, case-insensitive (`DockModel.launcherOf`). The same applications set a progress (a copy, a download): a thin bar near the bottom of the icon (`dock.progressHeight` 5, inset `dock.progressInset` 6 and offset `dock.progressBottomOffset` 4 from the icon, track the appearance fill colour (white in dark, black in light) at `dock.progressTrackOpacity` 0.4, fill the accent, so light and dark follow the appearance; Reduce Motion does not change it), shown while the progress is 0..1 and hidden at -1 (`DockModel.tileProgress`); it hops with the icon. The state is kept by `MenuBridge` (`launcher`, pure `MenuBridge.js` `applyLauncher`; tests `tests/unit/tst_menubridge.qml`, `tests/unit/tst_dockmodel.qml`) and cleared when the bridge disconnects: without the bridge the Dock behaves as before. Read-only; the Dock never clears notifications.
15. Drag to reorder: pressing and dragging an app tile moves it; neighbours make room; on release `DockModel.reorder` returns the new pinned list (a running or recent app dropped before a pinned one becomes pinned). Dragged more than `dock.dragOutDistance` (56) above the shelf top a pinned or recent app, running or not, shows a "Remove" capsule and, on release, leaves the Dock with a puff (`dock.poofDuration` 420, own drawing: fading circles) and is unpinned. A running app is removed too (macOS; owner report 2026-11-08, foot): it is unpinned, the puff plays, and its tile stays with its running dot in the running section until the app quits (it is then a recent app). Pin, match and removal compare ids in one spelling (`DockModel.normId`: no ".desktop", case-folded), so the stored pin always goes; an emptied list stays empty, nothing re-seeds the defaults (`migratePinned` only rewrites the exact old default). While a pinned or recent app is dragged out past the threshold, the shelf closes the gap of the tile (the shelf narrows by one pitch around its centre, the tiles after it follow, `motion.fast`) and reopens when the tile is dragged back; on release the tiles animate from where they were shown. Tiles are not rebuilt while a tile is dragged (a window opening or closing waits for the release, then the latest state is applied), so a release always finishes the drag. Drag-out removal follows "cancel on interruption" (owner decision, events only, no timing or hover heuristics, no plugin). One flag per drag (`dragInterrupted`) is false when the drag starts (a press already interrupted when the drag would start does not start it at all, see 13) and is set, until the release is handled, by: a shell full-attention surface opening (`FocusReturn.surfaceOpened`: Search, Apps, switcher, popup host, Notification Center, widget menu, Overview); any shell popup changing (`Popups.kind`: Dock menu, menu-bar menus, Control Center, ...); the session lock (`FocusReturn.locked`); keyboard mode; Hyprland events relayed by `Compositor.interrupted` (window open, close or move, active window, workspace created, destroyed, changed or moved, special workspace, fullscreen, focused monitor, monitor added or removed, a layer opening from any client, banners included: over-cancelling is accepted); the toplevel list or the output list changing (an app quitting); and Qt cancelling or stealing the grab. Only the Dock's own drag feedback (Remove capsule, puff, tooltip) is excluded: it lives in the Dock window and reports none of these. A tile is removed only when ALL hold: a positive Released event point ends the exclusive grab, that release is outside the drop zone with the live drag offset agreeing, the item can be removed, and the drag was not interrupted. Qt ends an interrupted drag with a synthesized release that looks like a real one (fresh timestamp, any position), so a real release alone is not enough. After an interruption the tile glides back and the user may have to drag again (accepted). Esc cannot be seen by the Dock during a mouse drag (it has no keyboard focus); it cancels through keyboard mode only. A release inside the shelf never removes. `tests/visual/dock-drag-stress.sh` repeats the drag-out-and-back pattern 50 times (every fifth with a window opening during the drag) and asserts the pinned list never changes (`qs ipc call dock pinned`); it also runs every interruption trigger with the tile held above the Dock window and inside it, still and moving, and the deliberate removals (release far away, top edge, corner, slow, hold, flick, leave and re-enter, inside the window outside the drop zone), which must all remove. The drag is clamped so that the "Remove" capsule stays inside the window (`dock.dragArea` 168). An interrupted drag changes no pins at all: neither the removal nor the reorder is applied (`finishDrag` glides the tile back whenever `dragInterrupted` is set), and a release without interruption reorders and unpins as above.
16. File drops (macOS: a tile accepts a drop only if the app can open that document): a tile highlights and accepts files (text/uri-list) only when the entry's Exec has a file field code (`%f %F %u %U`, read from the raw `DesktopEntry.execString` through `DockModel.execArgs`: Quickshell's `command` has the codes stripped) AND its `MimeType` matches the type of every file (type from the shared MIME database by file name, with subclasses and aliases; an entry without `MimeType` accepts nothing; `DockModel.acceptsFiles`, data from `dock/mime-data.sh` via `Apps.mimeData`).     - Tile: Display as Stack shows the newest items of the folder piled up (the pile is the first three of the folder in the stack's own order, the same order as the popup, both from one process: `StackWatch` runs ONE native helper per folder, `grassfield-stack-helper` (ADR-0025, `apps/stack-helper`, started by `StackHelper.qml`) and shows the first three of its window. The helper lists the folder itself (`readdir`, `statx` per entry where the order needs dates, no hidden names, links not followed, an item with its own dates, a broken link too; an entry that vanishes before its `statx` is skipped and the scan goes on, and a window entry that vanishes after it makes the scan run again, so a window is never made of files that are gone) and keeps only the WINDOW, the first 30 entries in the stack's order (3 shown plus 27 of margin; 300 while the popup is open, `K <n>` on its stdin), in a sorted vector. Name order is Files' by construction: the helper compiles `apps/files/src/filelogic.cpp` `compareEntries` with the `QCollator` that `FolderModel` sets (user locale, numeric, case insensitive, ties by the raw names): "file2" before "file10", "a" with "A", "e" before "é" before "f"; Date Added is the inode change time (the birth time when later; Linux has no better), the dates newest first, Kind folders first then by extension, ties by name. It watches with inotify itself (no inotifywait, no gawk). No decision rests on a count of the folder: every changed name is looked at in the folder as it is now (a name created and removed within one batch is simply not there, whatever the order of the events), a name that is there and sorts into the window is inserted with one comparison against the window's last entry, a name that sorts after it costs one comparison and nothing else. The window is always a correct prefix of the full sort: when entries of the folder may be outside it, a BOUNDARY entry says that every entry up to it is in the window and every other entry sorts after it. A name goes into the window only if it sorts up to the boundary, a name of the window that now sorts after it leaves the window, and a removal just shortens it: that is the MARGIN (the pile shows 3 of the 30 entries kept; a browser download, a visible temporary name that is renamed, and a deleted shown file cost no scan). Scans are at most once a second, coalesced, wait for 300 ms without events (5 s at most; a change of order or K at once) and are paid for in CPU time (not wall time, which a busy machine stretches) from a credit that lets scans use a fifth of it (8 s saved up at the start, so that the re-list after a quiet time is never held back); a scan that cannot be completed (a file kept vanishing under it) is thrown away and the window held stays. While a scan is due the window, still a correct prefix, keeps being written as long as at least half of `low` entries are left (what was written before holds entries that are gone); below that, and when the window may be wrong (overflow, storm, new order or size), it is not written and what was written before stays on show until the scan has made the new one complete, however long a loaded machine takes (a 100,000-name Date Added refill with eight busy loops took 11.8 s and wrote one window). In Date Added and Date Modified the sub-folders (up to 1,000) are watched too, and `IN_MODIFY` of the folder itself: a folder's date in the stack is its own modification (change) date, which moves when something is created, removed or renamed directly in it (macOS: the same), and a file that is written to and not closed yet has a new date at every write; changes deeper than one level, a hard link made elsewhere to a file of the folder (its change time moves, nothing is reported in this folder) and a sub-folder beyond the 1,000th (with more than 1,000 the folder is scanned again every 60 s, so the pile is at most a minute late for them) are seen at the next scan, not before; a renamed sub-folder keeps its watch under its new name; `IN_MODIFY` bursts (a download written in place) are looked at once per 500 ms;   the queue is read every 40 ms (not once per event: that cost 144 ticks for 20,000 creates and now costs about 30). A folder deleted, moved or unmounted is reported (`G`) and watched again after 2 s growing to 15 s (`A`, then a scan); the identity of the folder (device and inode of its path) is checked for every folder, once per batch of events and every 30 s while quiet (every 5 s for a symbolic link), and a change re-arms the watch and lists again: a file system mounted over the folder says nothing to inotify and is seen within 30 s. ## Dev notes: isolated app launches
In a `GRASSFIELD_DEV=1` session the shell runs on the owner's machine, so an app started from the Dock, Apps, Search, the Dock/app menus or by opening a desktop file must never use the owner's profiles (incident: Chromium launched from the nested Dock with the default profile). `Apps.launch(entryOrAction)` (`shell/services/Apps.qml`, the one launch path; helpers in `AppLaunch.js`, `tests/unit/tst_applaunch.qml`) starts the entry's `command` (Exec field codes dropped; `runInTerminal` through `xdg-terminal-exec`) with `Quickshell.execDetached({command, environment})` and `XDG_CONFIG_HOME`, `XDG_CACHE_HOME`, `XDG_STATE_HOME`, `XDG_DATA_HOME` set to `<dev>/apps-home/{config,cache,state,data}` (`<dev>` is the parent of `GRASSFIELD_STATE_HOME`, i.e. `.dev/`, which is ignored by version control). `XDG_DATA_DIRS` is kept so system desktop entries and icons resolve. The Dock Trash tile (`gio open trash:///`) and the Quick Settings Wi-Fi editor (`nm-connection-editor`) go through `Apps.openDetached` too. File openers (`gio open`, `xdg-open`, `gtk-launch` for desktop files and Search results) get the same environment through `Apps.openDetached` / `Apps.openEnvironment`. Outside a dev session `DesktopEntry.execute()` is called unchanged. Check: launch foot from the nested Dock, then `tr '\0' '\n' < /proc/<pid>/environ | grep XDG_`.

## Known limitations
- Real pointer hover, edge reveal and tooltips were checked through IPC test hooks and keyboard focus, not with a physical pointer (wtype is keyboard only).
- Window cycling order is the foreign-toplevel list order. Hidden (special workspace) windows are brought back by Dock click or Show All. Live thumbnails of minimized windows depend on the compositor still rendering windows parked on a special workspace; not verified (the nested run used the `balanced` profile, icon tiles).
- Dock icons come from the installed theme and are not Grassfield artwork.
- Stacks (requirement 27): "Date Added" is the inode change time (the `statx` change time, or the birth time when later: a download finishing or a file moved into the folder updates it, a `chmod` does too); Linux keeps no better "added to this folder" date (no `kMDItemDateAdded`, the Tracker/localsearch index has none either), so it stays so. The side-Dock Grid and List have no tail; the Fan's sideways bend and label side are fixed per edge (labels away from the bend); a folder tile takes no dropped files and cannot be dragged to reorder or off the Dock; dragging an item out of an open stack is not done; the insertion gap is computed from the unmagnified layout, so with magnification on it can be a few px off.
- The shrink to fit budgets the magnified shelf at its widest (requirement 26), so a Dock with many tiles and magnification on is smaller at rest than the same Dock without it.
- Drag and release were tested with `scripts/dev/pointer.sh` in ONE invocation (press, drag, release); a release sent from another invocation does not end the drag (a virtual pointer destroyed while pressed leaves the button down for the shell): a test harness artefact. The minimize animation toward the Dock is plugin work (not implemented); the fallback hides the window at once.
- Magnified icons grow away from the screen edge (upward for a Dock at the bottom); the shelf keeps its thickness and only lengthens.
- The Dock window is as deep as the largest size needs (requirement 25) and is transparent: it takes input only where its mask says (the shelf, the room around it while the pointer is on it, the 2 px edge strip while hidden).
- The shelf rim and highlight stay on its top edge for every position (macOS draws the Dock on the sides in the same material).
- Real pointer behaviour (edge dwell on a second output, the resize cursor, dragging the separator) is covered by the nested scenario `tests/visual/dock-geometry.sh`; the pure parts (every position formula, resize mapping, the choice of the Dock's output) by `tests/unit/tst_dockgeometry.qml`.

## What takes the pointer (owner report 2026-10-25, rc1: "an app is open, I click it in the Dock and nothing happens")
macOS model: the whole visible icon, magnified or not, is the target of the press, the hover (label), the drag, a file drop and both menus (press and hold, right click), from any point of it.
Root cause (measured on GF-REF with the owner's settings, magnification and auto-hide on): a tile box is `iconSize + indicator` deep across the Dock and the layout (a `Grid`) is made from those boxes, but a magnified icon grows across the Dock beyond its box (`Geometry.iconOrigin`: away from the screen edge). The pointer handlers (`TapHandler`, `HoverHandler`, `DragHandler`, `DropArea`) are scoped to their tile, so the outer `1 - 1/scale` of a magnified icon was dead: 37.5 % at 1.6, 60 % at 2.5. A click on the visible top of the icon (Apps at y = 988) logged nothing; at y = 1030 (inside the tile box) it opened. Along the Dock the boxes are laid out one after the other, so two tiles never share a point there, which is why clicks in the middle and at the bottom of an icon always worked.
Rule: every tile has a hit box, a child of the tile (`hit` in `Dock.qml`), that is the join of the tile box and the drawn icon (`Geometry.hitBox`); the handlers and drop areas of the tile are scoped to it (`parent: hit`), so it follows the hover rise and a drag. Along the Dock it is the tile's own extent (no new overlap); only the depth across the Dock grows. Where hit boxes do share a point (a tile being dragged, tiles making room) the tile drawn on top owns the press: highest `z`, then the later tile, as Qt stacks siblings (`Geometry.hitOwner`, `Dock.tileOwningPress`); the tap, the press and hold menu, the right click menu and the start of a drag check it. The layer window's input mask (`Geometry.hitRect`) is, with the pointer away from the Dock, the shelf (thickness + margin) only, so windows above the Dock keep their clicks; while the pointer is on the Dock it adds `overhang` (the growth of a magnified icon, 0 with magnification off) and the room for a label or a dragged icon (`Dock.tipRoom`: `dock.tooltipArea`, 44 px, for a Dock at the bottom, `dock.tooltipSideArea`, 240 px, for one on a side edge), also with magnification off; the whole window while a tile is dragged; the 2 px edge strip while the Dock is hidden.
Tests: `tests/unit/tst_dockgeometry.qml` (`test_hitBoxCoversTheWholeMagnifiedIcon`, `test_hitOwnerIsTheTileDrawnOnTop`); `tests/visual/dock-hit.sh` (real pointer clicks on GF-REF at the top, middle and bottom of a magnified icon, scales 1.6 and 2.5, Dock at the bottom, left and right, auto-hide and magnification on: each click taps exactly the tile whose drawn icon holds the pointer); `tests/visual/dock-drops.sh` (file drops).
